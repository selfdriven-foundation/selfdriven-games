Level 01 The old game

Work was always a game. Nobody called it that.

Objectives. Rules. Scores. Levels. Status. Teams. Rivals. You grind skills, complete challenges, level up, earn resources, build rep, unlock the next area. That's a career. It's also an RPG with worse graphics.

Old rules

Scarcity set the quests

Not enough food, land, money, knowledge, skill, time. When something is scarce, getting it matters. That's where the objectives came from — nobody had to design them.

Patch notes

Smart gets copied now

Human expertise can't be duplicated. Machine intelligence can. Once a capable system exists, the next thousand copies are cheap. That's a rule change civilisation has never had before.

Side effect

The levels get cleared for you

Brilliant news, mostly. But a lot of the challenges people built their lives around just... vanish. What happens when fewer things need us?

Boss fight The abundance paradox

Problems were doing a job we didn't notice.

A mountain creates the possibility of climbing it. A broken thing creates the possibility of fixing it. An unknown creates the possibility of finding out. Remove the obstacle and the suffering goes — but so does the challenge.

▶ Boss: The Abundance Paradox

The better we get at deleting problems nobody chose, the more it matters that we design challenges people do choose.

Weakness: voluntary difficulty  ·  Party required
Level 02 How games actually work

Every good game is made-up difficulty.

There is no law of physics that says a bishop moves diagonally. Someone invented that. The invented restriction is the whole reason the game exists.

Chess

Arbitrary on purpose

Nothing requires diagonal moves. The pointless rule is what creates somewhere interesting to think.

Football

Easy would ruin it

Just pick the ball up and walk it in. Simpler? Yes. Still a game? Not even slightly.

Golf

Difficulty by design

Walk over, drop the ball in the hole. Done. The whole sport is an agreement not to do that.

Old save file

“Here's a problem. Solve it or suffer.”

Objectives assigned by circumstance. Effort extracted by consequence. Failure expensive and often permanent.

New game+

“Here are a thousand challenges. Which one is worth it?”

You pick the objective. Effort becomes self-directed. Failure is survivable — and that's what makes it useful.

Level 03 No cheat codes

An AI can't play your run for you.

A chess engine has flattened every human for decades. People still play chess. Calculators exist. People still learn maths. Cars are faster than you. People still run marathons. Sometimes the output is the player.

Quest board Real world, real XP

Stop asking “what's your job”. Ask what you're working on.

A game is just a tiny governance system: who's playing, what counts, what's allowed, how you settle disputes, how progress gets measured. Point that at the real world and you get quests that actually leave something behind.

▶ Tap a quest to accept it. Your identity stops being your occupation and starts being your participation.

Level 05 The scoreboard

Whatever you score is what you become.

Most games built in the last decade score attention and spending. That's a design choice, and a bad one. Score something else and you get a different kind of player.

▶ Badly designed game

▶ Well designed game

▶ Rule of thumb

The scoreboard isn't the point. It's just there to show you the journey.

Level 06 The new scarcity

Abundance doesn't end scarcity. It moves it.

When intelligence, information and compute get cheap, something else becomes the rare drop: actual human engagement. You can't mass-produce attention. You can only care deeply about so many things.

purposeattentiontrust belongingreputationbeing real friendshipsbeing there in person shared historyagencytime

None of that ships with another GPU.

Level ∞ The infinite game

There is no final level of curiosity.

Finite games have a prize and a winner. Infinite games just keep going — knowledge, science, friendship, creativity, exploration, community, getting better at something. No maximum amount of caring. No last piece of music. No final question. No last friendship worth having.

The risk isn't nothing to do.

There will always be things that can be done. The real question is whether you've got things you actually want to do — and that's a design problem. That's what selfdriven.games is for.
